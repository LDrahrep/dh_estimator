Site condition multipliers · applied to labor

Applied to the labor lines only: racks, links, trunks, pathways and certification. Documentation and mobilization stay flat. When several conditions are on, the multipliers compound:

Conditionmultiplier
Live data hallWorking next to the customer's powered equipment: escorts and permits, no dust or noise, aisles stay clear, every cable is pulled slower and with more care, and a slip can take down someone else's link. Usually the heaviest multiplier.
Pathways above 3 mA lift or scaffold, fall protection and a second person on the floor. Fewer meters of tray per hour.
Night windows, weekendsCrew premium for nights and weekends, short work windows with idle time between them, after-hours site access.
Under raised floorLifting and replacing tiles, working prone, cable is harder to see and slower to label than in an open overhead tray.

Not included

  • Active equipment and its configuration.
  • Cable, trunks, trays and hardware as materials: separate bill of materials.
  • Power up to the PDU, busway, UPS.
  • General contractor scope: floor, ceiling, fire suppression.
  • Rigging through non-standard openings.

What narrows the bracket

± %Customer's word. Rack count, ports per rack, media type, new or live hall.
± %Hall floor plan. Rows, meet-me room, route lengths, meters of tray, length bands.
± %Port-to-port schedule. Cable and bundle schedule, firm price.

What lowers the price

  • Pre-terminated trunks instead of field termination.
  • Racks pre-built with PDUs and sensors at the factory.
  • Daytime access before the hall goes live.
  • Overhead pathways instead of raised floor.
  • Staging at or near the site instead of hauling every load from the warehouse.
  • A floor plan before the first meeting: straight to the second level of estimate.
